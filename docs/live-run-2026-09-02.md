## What it found

Verdict `needs_changes`, five findings. Three of them are the ones the
deterministic rules also catch:

- the hampers hold wine and pork salami, barring Elpida, Anemos and the School
- the biscuits hold hazelnut, and Anemos is nut free for a child with a severe
  allergy

**Two of them the rules do not catch, and this is the part worth reading.**

1. **Undeclared milk and gluten.** The offer declares no allergens. The manifest
   has hard cheese in the hampers and pasta, crackers and biscuits throughout.
   `_MANIFEST_TOKENS` in `fleet.py` covers alcohol, pork and nuts, because those
   are the three somebody thought to write down. The model read the manifest
   rather than matching against a list, so it found the other two.

2. **The offer contradicts the policy, and no rule compares them.** The donor
   requires the whole 180 units moved in one collection. The allocation policy
   caps any one member at 40%, which is 72 units. No member may take the lot, so
   the donor's condition and the network's own rule cannot both hold. Nothing in
   this repository checks an offer's conditions against the policy. That is a
   gap the model found in the fixture, not a gap the fixture was built to have.

It also proposed a workable resolution rather than only refusing: Omonoia has
two vans and no premises constraints, so it can collect the whole lot and keep
every restricted line itself at exactly 72 units, and the barred goods never
reach the other four premises.

## And what it said it could not determine

Quoted, because this is the behaviour that is usually missing:

> One gap I could not close: the filing holds no allocation records, so I could
> not verify who received the earlier ambient offer 4471. Only one prior ambient
> offer exists, so no one can be sent to the back of the queue on this evidence,
> but that is unconfirmed, not cleared.

Absent evidence reported as absent, rather than as a pass. The prompt asks for
that, so this is the instruction working rather than a surprise, but a run that
had quietly treated an empty rota as a clean rota would have looked identical in
a summary count.

## The honest costs

**104.6 seconds for one specialist.** Four specialists is roughly seven minutes,
which is why the reader's Lambda timeout is 900 seconds and not the 300 it was
when this was measured. A judge curling `/run` waits minutes, and that is a real
cost of an agent that reads rather than matches.

**10 reads of 12.** The bound held and the run stopped short of it. The budget
was not the thing that ended the run.

**One run is not a measurement.** This is one offer, one specialist, one model,
once. It is evidence that the path works and that reading changes the answer. It
is not a claim about reliability, and nothing here has been run enough times to
support one.
